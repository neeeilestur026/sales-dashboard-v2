THE CORRUPTED FIGURE REACHED INVENTORY, AND CORRECTING THE PAYABLE DID NOT UNDO IT
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  MR-202608-014 was received on 2026-08-09 WHILE AP-202607-006 still held ₱310,895.71. createReceiving
  read that figure through _apPaidPHP and stamped it across the two lines as their landed cost, then
  called _applyInventory to fold it into the weighted-average cost of CP872 and CP873.

  Correcting the AP row afterwards fixed the payable and the ledger. It did NOT fix inventory:
  receiving is a one-time stamp, and there is NO deleteReceiving anywhere in FlowAPI.gs.

  CP872 and CP873 therefore carry a cost basis roughly 25x too high (₱310,895.71 against a USD 202
  order, i.e. ~₱12,447.24). The moment either is invoiced, that inflated figure becomes COGS and the
  gross profit on that sale is badly understated.

  Correct landed cost for the two lines, recomputed from the corrected payable:
      purchase total = 12,447.24  (split between CP872/CP873 by unit price over PO total FC)
      + inventoriable shipping 3,120.11
      = 15,567.35 across 2 units, versus the 314,015.82 currently stamped.

  OVERSTATEMENT IN INVENTORY: 310,895.71 - 12,447.24 = 298,448.47

  This is the SAME ₱298,448.47 that appears as the false Cash credit in tests/flow/baseline/
  A221-before.txt. One mistyped cell produced it three times: in the payable, in the general ledger,
  and in the inventory cost basis.
