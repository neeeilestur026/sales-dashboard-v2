WHAT THIS TABLE DECIDED ABOUT THE RULE
--------------------------------------
The `approvedPRsum` column is the fact the company actually has — no exchange rate is involved.
Reading it against `amountPHP` gives a rule that needs no rate at all:

    A payable marked Paid cannot exceed what was actually paid for it.

  · AP-001 Paid, 446,393.80 vs 44,323.20 paid  -> FLAG (10x)
  · AP-006 Paid, 310,895.71 vs 12,447.24 paid  -> FLAG (25x)
  · AP-007 Paid, 30,240 vs 30,240              -> passes. The VAT is on the PR too, so the
                                                  12% uplift needs no special case whatsoever.
  · AP-009 / AP-011 Partial                    -> rule does not apply; a part payment is
                                                  legitimately less than the payable.
  · AP-012 / AP-013 Unpaid                     -> rule does not apply.
  · AP-014 Unpaid (SGD)                        -> rule does not apply; its rate is stored and right.

That is exactly the two known-bad rows and nothing else, from double-entry alone rather than from any
opinion about what an exchange rate should be. The 20-200 FX band survives only as a weak typo net
for rows that have no payment yet, where there is nothing better to check against.
